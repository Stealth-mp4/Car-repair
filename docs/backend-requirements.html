Admin Dashboard — Backend Requirements

What the dashboard does today, and the external services it needs before the shop can run on it day to day.

Prepared for: Iqballaz Customs Date: 5 August 2026 Status: Front end complete, sample data

1. Where things stand

The admin dashboard is fully built and clickable. Every screen from the approved reference design exists and works: the dashboard overview with revenue chart and figures, an appointments calendar with month, week and day views, and thirteen management screens covering customers, vehicles, projects, services, invoices, payments, finance, reviews, messages, users, staff, inventory and an activity log. You can add, edit and delete records, search, sort, filter and page through them, and book or reschedule from the calendar.

What it is not doing yet is remembering anything. All of it currently runs on sample data held in the browser, so a page refresh resets it. Nothing is stored, no card is charged, and no customer is emailed or texted. That is by design — the interface was built first so it could be reviewed and signed off before the plumbing behind it is commissioned.

Making it real means connecting a small number of external services. This document lists each one: what it is for, what it costs, and what we need from you to set it up.

For context: this is an addition to your existing website, not a replacement. The site is already live and already hosted, and it will keep deploying the way it does now — the dashboard simply becomes part of it. Nothing in this document requires rebuilding or moving the site.

2. Essential — the dashboard cannot go live without these

Phase 1. Everything else depends on this group. Hosting already exists — the entry below is about access, not cost.

2.1 Hosting — already in place

The site is already live and deploys from your copy of the project, which you sync when we ship changes. That arrangement does not change, and there is nothing new to buy or set up.

One thing to plan for: each service in this document is switched on by adding a small number of environment variables — settings such as a database address or an API key — in the hosting dashboard. They deliberately live outside the code and are never stored in the project files, which is what keeps them private. Syncing the repository does not carry them across.

So as each service goes live, someone needs to add those values. Either you add them from a list we provide, or we are given access to the hosting project and add them directly — whichever you prefer. It is only worth agreeing which before Phase 1 starts.

Requires
Nothing new — no additional account, no additional cost
Indicative cost
None, beyond your current hosting plan
We may need
Access to the hosting project, or a named person on your side who can add environment variables when we send them
Note
We understand the site is hosted on Vercel — worth confirming, as the place to enter these settings differs slightly between providers

2.2 Database

The single most important item. This is the shop’s permanent record: customers, vehicles, appointments, projects, invoices, payments, inventory and staff. Without it, nothing the dashboard shows survives a refresh.

It also becomes the one source of truth shared with the customer Vehicle Passport already on the public site, so a customer’s warranty and service history stops being hand-maintained in two places.

Recommended
Supabase or Neon (both managed PostgreSQL, with automatic backups)
Alternatives
Vercel Postgres, Amazon RDS
Indicative cost
Free tier covers early use; approx. $19–25/month once past it
We need
An account in the shop’s name. Also a decision on where data is stored geographically, and how long records are kept
Note
Please budget for this even if usage stays inside a free tier — free tiers pause inactive databases, which is not appropriate for live business records

2.3 Staff accounts and permissions

Today the dashboard has a proper sign-in screen, but behind it sits a single shared password. That means no individual staff logins, no password resets, and no record of who changed what.

The Users and Staff screens already show access levels — Super Admin, Manager, Technician, Front Desk — but nothing enforces them yet. Once this is connected, those levels become real: a technician would not be able to see finance, for instance.

Recommended
Auth.js (no licence fee, runs on the shop’s own hosting) or Clerk (paid, but includes ready-made password reset and two-factor)
Indicative cost
Auth.js: free. Clerk: free to roughly 10,000 monthly users, then approx. $25/month
We need
The list of staff who should have access and the level each one gets. A decision on whether two-factor sign-in is required
Recommendation: commission Phase 1 as a single block. The database and the staff accounts are interdependent, and both need their settings added to the hosting at the same time — splitting them means paying twice to wire the same connections.

3. Operating — needed for daily use

Phase 2. Turns the dashboard from a record book into the tool the shop runs on.

3.1 Card payments

The Invoices and Payments screens list what is owed and what has been settled, but nothing charges a card today. Connecting a payment provider lets an invoice be sent as a payment link, and marks itself paid automatically when the customer pays — no manual reconciliation.

Recommended
Stripe (already referenced on the public site’s financing page)
Alternatives
Square, PayPal
Indicative cost
No monthly fee. Roughly 2.9% + $0.30 per card transaction — confirm current US rates at signup
We need
A Stripe account with business verification completed, and the shop’s bank details for payouts
Note
The existing financing partners (Acima, Snap) stay a separate manual process unless they provide a direct integration — worth asking them

3.2 Email and text messaging

For appointment confirmations, reminders the day before, and “your vehicle is ready” notices. Given the shop books by appointment with same-week slots, text messages will matter more than email for reducing no-shows.

Recommended
Resend or Postmark for email; Twilio for text messages
Indicative cost
Email: free to roughly 3,000 messages/month, then approx. $20/month. Text: approx. $0.008 per message plus about $1.15/month for a number — confirm current rates
We need
Permission to send from the shop’s domain (a small DNS change), the sending address to use, and approval of the message wording. US text messaging also requires business registration with the carriers, which takes a few days

3.3 Enquiries into HubSpot — account already in place

The quote form and the chat assistant on the public site already collect enquiries correctly, in a single consistent format. At present they are only written to the server log. They will be sent to your existing HubSpot account instead, so every enquiry becomes a contact and a deal in the CRM the shop already uses.

The dashboard’s Messages screen will show the same enquiries, so the shop can respond from either place. HubSpot stays the system of record for the sales pipeline; the dashboard is the day-to-day working view.

Requires
Your HubSpot account (already in place) plus the database (2.2)
Indicative cost
None beyond your existing HubSpot plan — the free CRM tier covers contacts and forms
We need
A HubSpot private-app token with contact and deal permissions, and a decision on which pipeline and deal stage new enquiries should land in

3.4 AI chat assistant — Claude (Anthropic)

The chat widget on the public site works today, but it is a scripted assistant: it answers from a fixed set of questions via tap-through buttons, and hands off to a human for anything else. It cannot answer a question phrased in the customer’s own words.

Connecting it to Claude turns it into a real assistant: it answers free-text questions about services, materials, turnaround and financing in the shop’s own voice, and still hands off to a human — and into HubSpot — when the customer wants a quote or a booking.

Requires
Your existing Anthropic account, with credits added
Indicative cost
Billed per conversation, not monthly. At the volume a single shop sees, roughly $10–40/month on the recommended model. Start with a $50 credit and review after the first month
We need
Credits added to the Anthropic account and an API key. Plus approval of what the assistant may and may not say — most importantly whether it quotes prices, or only ever gives ranges and refers to the shop
Note
Cost scales with how many people chat, so it is worth setting a monthly spend cap in the Anthropic console from day one

3.5 Model choice and cost control

Anthropic offers several models at different price points. The difference the shop would notice is answer quality on unusual or detailed questions — all three are fast enough for a chat widget. Pricing below is per million words-worth of text, roughly; a typical customer conversation is a very small fraction of that.

Model Best for Input / output per 1M tokens
Claude Opus 5 (recommended) Best answer quality; handles detailed and unusual questions well $5 / $25
Claude Sonnet 5 Strong quality at lower cost; a sensible middle option $3 / $15
Claude Haiku 4.5 Cheapest and fastest; fine for simple, repetitive questions $1 / $5

Prices are Anthropic’s published rates and should be confirmed at signup. The model can be changed at any time without rebuilding the assistant, so it is easy to start on one and move if the quality or the cost is not right. Repeated instructions are cached automatically, which cuts the real cost well below a naive per-message estimate.

3.6 File storage

For invoice PDFs and customer build photographs. These currently point at fixed files in the website folder, which does not scale and cannot be added to from the dashboard. Proper storage also allows private links, so a customer’s invoice is not publicly guessable.

Recommended
Cloudflare R2 (no charge for data transfer out) or Amazon S3
Indicative cost
Typically under $5/month at this scale
We need
An account, and a decision on how long customer photos are retained

4. Enhancement — valuable, not urgent

Phase 3. Can follow once the shop is running on the dashboard.

4.1 Google review sync

The Reviews screen currently holds reviews typed in by hand. Connecting Google Business Profile pulls them in automatically, so the screen and the website’s review section always reflect reality.

Requires
Google Business Profile / Places API access
Indicative cost
Charged per request; low volume typically sits within the monthly free allowance — confirm current terms
We need
Admin access to the shop’s Google Business Profile

4.2 Reporting figures

The comparison figures on the dashboard — “+12% from last week”, “+15% revenue” — are sample numbers. Once real records accumulate, these become genuine period-on-period calculations from the shop’s own data.

Requires
Nothing new — calculated from the database (2.2)
Indicative cost
None
We need
Confirmation of which figures matter most to the business, so the right ones are shown

4.3 Editable settings

The Settings screen currently displays business details, opening hours and the live promotion, but they are read-only — those values live in the website’s code, and showing editable boxes would imply a save that goes nowhere. Once stored in the database, the shop can change opening hours or swap the promotion without a developer.

Requires
Nothing new — depends on the database (2.2)
Indicative cost
None

6. What we need from you

Work can begin as soon as these are available. Accounts should be created in the shop’s name, not a developer’s, so ownership stays with the business.

Already in place — we just need access

  • Hosting — either access to the project, or a named person who can add environment variables as each service is connected
  • HubSpot — a private-app token with contact and deal permissions
  • Anthropic — credits added to the account, plus an API key

Accounts still to open

  • Database provider (Phase 1)
  • Stripe, with business verification and bank details (Phase 2)
  • Email and text messaging providers (Phase 2)
  • File storage provider (Phase 2)

Access to grant

  • DNS access for iqballazcustoms.com — only to authorise sending email from the domain (the site itself is already pointed correctly)
  • Admin access to the Google Business Profile, for review sync (Phase 3)

Decisions to confirm

  • Which staff get dashboard access, and at what level
  • Which HubSpot pipeline and deal stage new enquiries should land in
  • What the chat assistant may say about pricing — exact quotes, ranges only, or refer to the shop
  • Which Claude model to run, and the monthly spend cap to set
  • Whether two-factor sign-in is required for staff
  • Wording for appointment confirmations and reminders
  • How long customer records and photographs are retained
  • Who is notified when a new website enquiry arrives

Information to verify

The dashboard is currently populated with realistic sample data so the screens could be reviewed properly. The following are placeholders and must be replaced with real figures before launch:

  • Staff names and roles
  • Service pricing and typical duration
  • Inventory items, suppliers and reorder levels
  • The finance ledger and all revenue figures
On the sample data: none of the customers, vehicles, invoices or payments currently shown are real. They exist purely to make the screens legible during review and will be cleared before the shop starts using the dashboard.

7. Suggested sequence

Phase Delivers Outcome for the shop
1 Database and staff accounts (hosting already in place) The dashboard becomes real: records are kept, staff have their own logins, and the customer Vehicle Passport shares the same data
2 Payments, email and text, HubSpot lead capture, AI chat assistant, file storage The shop can run its day on it: take payment, confirm and remind customers, capture every enquiry in HubSpot, and let the chat assistant answer real questions around the clock
3 Review sync, reporting, editable settings Reviews stay current without typing, figures reflect real trading, and the shop can update its own details

Phase 1 is the meaningful milestone — at the end of it the dashboard stops being a demonstration and becomes the shop’s system of record. Phases 2 and 3 add capability on top of a working foundation and can be scheduled to suit budget.
